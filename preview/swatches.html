Flame Swatches

Every colour and opacity the fire is actually drawn with, on the app's own black. Look at this at your normal screen brightness, ideally in a dim room.

For each row, find the leftmost column you can still see and remember its number. That is where your panel stops separating the fire from the background — and it is the number I need, not a judgement about whether it looks good.

Flame layers

Violet through red, the seven stops of the ramp. Marked columns are the three layer opacities in use: the outer halo, the middle layer, and the core.

The glow

A gradient, not a flat fill, so it is shown as drawn. Left is silence, right is a loud passage. If the fire loses its halo at normal brightness, it shows up here first.

White reference

The same alphas in plain white. If white survives at a column where the colours do not, the panel is crushing colour rather than brightness — a different problem with a different fix. The stars are drawn in this range too.

Screenshots of this page cannot answer the question: a screenshot stores the values, and brightness is applied after them by the panel. Only your eyes on the real screen, or a photograph of it with exposure locked, can.
